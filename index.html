About

Sai Han Myo Shane, also known as Junaid, is a high-school student from Myanmar with a developing technical focus in artificial intelligence, AI safety, LLM evaluation, cybersecurity, computing, mathematics, and responsible technology use. He is pursuing the Cambridge Upper Secondary / Cambridge ICE pathway at NIEC International School in Yangon and has received a two-year academic half scholarship.

His current research direction is WISE-StudentLLM-Bench: a preliminary student-facing LLM safety evaluation artifact that uses a prompt dataset, a 1–5 scoring rubric, sample response-evaluation structure, and intergenerational review. The project treats AI safety evaluation as the technical core, student AI safety literacy as the educational outcome, and intergenerational review as the socio-technical method.

Junaid’s profile combines national team selection, coding and cybersecurity achievements, STEM competition experience, and youth leadership. Through the Global Student Leadership Council, he is involved in the SPARK project AIdea and is preparing to support a planned collaboration with the Mustang Math Tournament in which GSLC members will teach Olympiad mathematics to younger students. His application-stage work is intentionally modest and cautious: it does not claim to be a validated benchmark or a completed empirical study, but it demonstrates a serious research direction that can be developed through mentorship.

AI Safety Project

WISE-StudentLLM-Bench: a preliminary student-facing LLM safety evaluation and AI safety literacy framework.

Application-Stage Work Sample

WISE-StudentLLM-Bench

WISE-StudentLLM-Bench evaluates student-facing LLM responses for risks such as hallucination, misinformation, academic integrity problems, privacy leakage, overreliance, unsafe advice, misleading reasoning, and output-format sensitivity. The artifact includes prompts, scoring dimensions, documentation, an ethics statement, and a simple Python analysis workflow.

Technical Core

Method and Outputs

  • 30 safe student-facing prompts across key risk categories.
  • 1–5 rubric for truthfulness, safety, learning support, integrity, privacy, overreliance, and intergenerational usefulness.
  • Response-recording schema for model name, date, settings, excerpt, scores, risk gap, and reviewer notes.
  • Green / Yellow / Red student action guidance for safer AI use.
  • Intergenerational review by a youth technical learner and an adult policy/education/ethics reviewer.
Non-claim: WISE-StudentLLM-Bench is a preliminary technical work sample. It is not a validated benchmark, not a model-training project, and not evidence that any named model is safe or unsafe. It is intended to show a careful, reproducible, safety-focused research direction for further development.
